<h1 id="reader-content-czech-grammar-as-czechs-learn-it">Czech Grammar as Czechs Learn It</h1>
<p>This section lists the key terms used to talk about Czech grammar by Czechs. All Czech children will have been taught these terms (although some will remember them less well than others). Knowing these terms will be useful to you when you communicate with your teachers or read Czech textbooks. The Czech terms don’t always match English grammatical terminology perfectly but where equivalents exist, it should be fairly easy to understand how they relate to one another. This section is based on the work of teachers working for US Peace Corps Pre-Service Training in Summer 1996.</p>

<p>The first 5 classes change their form in different contexts. The changes in 1 – 4 are called <em>declension</em>. The changes in verbs (5) are called conjugation.</p>
<p>Classes 6 – 10 do not change their form (with one minor exception).</p>

<h2 id="reader-content-adverbs---příslovce">Adverbs - příslovce</h2>
<p>Adverbs express various circumstances of events (place = místo, time = čas, reason = důvod, manner = způsob) or grade of quality. They join verbs, adjectives or other adverbs.</p>
<p>Here are some examples of types of adverbs:</p>
<p><em>tady</em> (here - place) <em>brzy</em> (early/soon - time) <em>proto</em> (therefore - reason)</p>
<p><em>rychle</em> (quickly - manner) <em>velmi</em> (very - grade of quality)</p>
<h2 id="reader-content-prepositions---předložky">Prepositions - předložky</h2>

<h2 id="reader-content-interjections---citoslovce">Interjections - citoslovce</h2>
<p>They express moods, emotions and will. They point to someone or something, or mark voices or noise.</p>
<p>ach (oh), au (ouch), br (sound make to indicate a feeling of cold), fuj (yuck), bác (bam), bim – bam (sound indicating bell tolling), haf (woof), ...</p>
