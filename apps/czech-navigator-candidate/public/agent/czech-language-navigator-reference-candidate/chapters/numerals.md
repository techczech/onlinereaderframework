<h2 id="reader-content-decimals-and-fractions">Decimals and Fractions</h2>
<p>Decimals are written with a comma as in <strong>1,5</strong> which is pronounced <strong>celá</strong> (whole) as in <strong>jedna celá pět</strong>.</p>
<p>Decimals between 0 and 1 are always pronounced with zero as in 0,2 <strong>nula celá dva</strong>.</p>
<p>Fraction names are formed by adding the ending <strong>–ina</strong> to the number.</p>
<blockquote>
<p>pět pět<strong>ina</strong></p>
<p>deset deset<strong>ina</strong></p>
</blockquote>
<p>Fractions formed from 2 - 4 have irregular forms</p>
<blockquote>
<p>dva <strong>polovina</strong> (half)</p>
<p>tři tř<strong>etina</strong> (third)</p>
<p>čtyři čt<strong>vrtina</strong> (quarter)</p>
</blockquote>

<h2 id="reader-content-case-and-numbers">Case and numbers</h2>
<p>Yes. Regrettably, all the Czech numbers have cases. Therefore, you use a different form when you say <em>I'll have <strong>two</strong> dumplings</em> (dám si dva knedlíky) and <em>I'll have it <strong>with</strong> two dumplings</em> (dám si to se dvěma knedlíky.) Also the endings are different for <strong><em>one</em></strong>, and <strong><em>two, three, four</em></strong> and <em><strong>five</strong> and more</em>.</p>

<h3 id="reader-content-cardinal-numbers-5-and-more">Cardinal numbers 5 and more</h3>
<p>Numbers from <strong>5</strong> through <strong>99</strong> use the ending <strong><em>-i</em></strong> for all cases except for <em>Koho? Co?</em> which stays the same as <em>Kdo? Co?</em></p>
<p>In compound numbers all the components have the ending. E.g. <em>s dvacet<strong>i</strong> pět<strong>i</strong>, bez dvacet<strong>i</strong> <strong>dvou</strong></em>.</p>
<p>100 (<strong>sto</strong>) has the same endings as <em>pivo</em> but doesn't change in daily spoken Czech</p>
<p>1 000 (<strong>tisíc</strong>) has the same endings as <em>čaj</em></p>
<p>1 000 000 (<strong>milión</strong>) has the same endings as <em>rohlík</em></p>
<p>1 000 000 000 (<strong>miliarda</strong>) has the same endings as <em>žena</em></p>
<h3 id="reader-content-other-numbers">Other numbers</h3>
<p><em>Ordinal</em> numbers (first, second, etc.) behave as adjectives: (<em>první, druhý, třetí, …</em>)</p>
<p>The <em>names</em> of numbers behave as feminine nouns: (<em>jednička, dvojka, trojka,</em> …)</p>
<p><em>Multiplication</em> numbers do not change their forms with case: (<em>jednou, dvakrát</em>, <em>třikrát, …</em>)</p>
<p><em>Generic</em> numbers don't often have to change their forms: (<em>jedny, dvoje, troje, …</em>) but if necessary they will adopt the forms of the plural of feminine nouns ending in <em>-a</em>.</p>
<h3 id="reader-content-number-names">Number names</h3>
<p>Numbers have special forms for their names. These might be used for instance when referring to numbered trains, players or grades. In English, just the number or the word Number is used.</p>
<p>The number name is formed using the ending <strong>-ka</strong>. This ending often modifies the form of the preceding number. See table above for exceptions.</p>
<p>9, 10, 20, 30, 40 <strong>-ítka</strong> (replaces <strong>-et</strong> as in dev<strong>ět</strong> - dev<strong>ítka</strong>)</p>
<p>33 second number is pronounced first followed by <strong>a</strong> as in <strong>třiatřicítka</strong></p>
<p>450 <strong>-ka</strong> is only added to the last number as in <strong>čtyřistapadesátka</strong></p>
<p>200 – 900 the first number uses the Koho? Čeho? form as in 500 = <strong>pětistovka</strong></p>

<h2 id="reader-content-dates-or-kolikátého-je">Dates or <em>Kolikátého je?</em></h2>
<p>Month is given second with both numeral followed by a period. E.g. 14<sup>th</sup> of January 1999 – 14. 1. 1999.</p>
<p>Both the ordinal number and the name of the month are in <em>Koho? Čeho?</em> case. If the numeral has two parts, such as twenty-two, both parts are changed.</p>
<p>The endings used for ordinal numbers are those of adjectives in <em>Koho? Čeho?</em> and the endings used for names of months are those of nouns.</p>
<p>The question used for asking for the date is: <em>Kolikátého je?</em></p>
