<h1 id="reader-content-pronouns">Pronouns</h1>
<h2 id="reader-content-personal-pronouns">Personal Pronouns</h2>
<p>Personal pronouns have different forms after prepositions, and at the beginning of sentences. If they are used independently, an unstressed form is used. Prepositions, however, require a stressed form. Já, ty, on have a special form used for when the noun is at the beginning of the sentence or when it is stressed.</p>

<h2 id="reader-content-possessive-pronouns">Possessive Pronouns</h2>
<p>Possessive pronouns express the relationship of possession, such as <em>my, your, …, their friend, friends</em>. In Czech, each possessive pronoun has several forms marking both the case and gender of the following noun. Note, also, that Czech possessive pronouns do not have a stressed form like the English <em>mine, yours, …</em>.</p>

<h2 id="reader-content-svůj-a-tricky-possessive-pronoun">SVŮJ a tricky possessive pronoun</h2>
<p>This pronoun is a rough equivalent of the English <em>my own, ..., their own</em>. It is only used when the possessor is also the subject of the sentence. This allows us to differentiate two meanings of <em>He went to visit his brother</em>.: <em>Šel navštívit <strong>jeho</strong> bratra.</em> (somebody else's) or <em>Šel navštívit <strong>svého</strong> bratra.</em> (his own)</p>

<h3 id="reader-content-other-prefixes">Other prefixes</h3>
<p>There are also other prefixes that can be used with these words. For example:</p>
<p><em>lec-/leda(s)</em> (just any, many) – <em>ledakdo</em> (just anybody)<em>, leckde</em> (just anywhere)</p>
<p><em>kde-</em> (pretty much any) – <em>kdekdo</em>, <em>kdeco</em> (cannot be used with <em>kde</em>) (colloquial)</p>
<p><em>málo-</em> (few) of <em>málokdo</em> (few people), <em>málokdy</em> (not often).</p>
<h3 id="reader-content-suffixes">Suffixes</h3>
<p>All of these words can also be used with suffixes which modify their meaning in various ways. <em>Jaký</em> and <em>který</em> take a regular ending before this suffix (e.g. <em>s jakýmsi</em> – with some…).</p>
<p>–<em>koli</em> (–ever) as in <em>kdokoli</em> (whoever), <em>kdekoli</em> (wherever), <em>jakýkoli</em> (whichever) etc.</p>
<p><em>–si</em> (some–) used as the archaic equivalent of <em>ně</em>– as in <em>kdosi</em> (someone), <em>cosi</em> (something), <em>kdesi</em> (somewhere), etc. <em>Kdysi</em> is now used mostly in the meaning of <em>long time ago</em>.</p>
<p><em>–pak</em> (quizzical enquiry) as in <em>kdopak</em> (who might it be), <em>copak</em>, <em>kdypak</em> (very colloquial)</p>
<h2 id="reader-content-reflexive-pronouns">Reflexive pronouns</h2>
