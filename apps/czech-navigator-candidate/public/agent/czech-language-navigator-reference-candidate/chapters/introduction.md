<h1 id="reader-content-introduction">Introduction</h1>
<h2 id="reader-content-what-this-is">What this is</h2>
<p>The Czech Language Navigator is an overview of the key points important in the study of Czech as a second language. It is organized in a way that will be familiar to most students of language but it also contains sections that go beyond grammar.</p>
<h2 id="reader-content-core-grammar-sections">Core grammar sections</h2>
<p>The first nine sections outline the core parts of speech in detail. These are followed by sections on Word Order, Pronunciation and Spelling. These sections focus on the things traditionally found in grammar books. They describe the how the different parts of speech are formed and how they are used in phrases and sentences.</p>
<h2 id="reader-content-sections-on-usage">Sections on usage</h2>
<p>This is followed by a section that covers the different ways in which Czech expresses itself going beyond grammar. Things like idioms, common ways of speaking and various differences between Czech and English. These things often fall between grammar books and dictionaries and may be scattered across various textbooks. The Navigator tries to present them more systematically.</p>
<h2 id="reader-content-sections-with-useful-background">Sections with useful background</h2>
<p>The next three sections provide useful background about Czech to learners and travelers. There is a section on the differences between standard and non-standard Czech. This is followed by a section of trivia related to Czech such as the history of the language that a student can use to enrich their understanding of the context in which the language is used.</p>
<p>The final section is an overview of the Czech grammatical system that is taught in Czech schools. Many Czechs will be familiar with the structure and terminology and conversations about the Czech language will be easier.</p>
