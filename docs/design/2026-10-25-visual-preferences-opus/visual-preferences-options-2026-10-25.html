Reading assistance and formatting: three arrangements

Each sketch is a small working model of the shared book reader, with real text from the three books. Choose a book, then open the two menus and change the settings: the text in the frame responds. Yellow notes are design annotations, not part of the reader. Nothing here plays sound.

Recommended: A. Two labelled menus in the existing toolbar, Reading assistance and Formatting, each opening below its button so most of the text stays visible while you change it. It keeps the toolbar you liked, brings back the icons and rounded controls of the original guides, and removes the duplicate and conflicting settings.

Book
A

Two menus in the toolbar

The toolbar keeps its place and gains two labelled menus. Reading assistance holds listening, focus aids and hiding the navigation. Formatting holds theme, typeface, size, spacing and width. Each opens below its button; a number on the button shows how many of its settings differ from the book's defaults. On a phone the same two menus rise from the bottom of the screen, with a switch between them at the top.

  • Closest to the current reader: only the toolbar and the settings window change.
  • Two separate menus answer the request directly, and each is short enough to fit without scrolling on a laptop screen.
  • Listen moves into Reading assistance. While the browser voice is reading, a small player with Pause and Stop appears in the toolbar.
  • Weakness: Listen is one click deeper than today.
B

One panel with two tabs

One Reading settings button opens a panel on the right with two tabs, Reading assistance and Formatting. Listen stays a separate toolbar button. The panel stays open while you read, so you can try settings against a whole page of text.

  • Everything in one place, with room for a short explanation under each setting.
  • Listen keeps its own button, so audio stays one click away.
  • Weakness: on a laptop the panel covers the "On this page" column, and the second tab is one extra click.
  • Weakness: it is the most like a settings window, the least like the original guides.
C

A floating bar, as in the original guide

The toolbar is removed. A rounded bar floats at the top right of the text with Listen, Assist, Format and Notes, the way the original Deliberate Practice guide placed its dark mode, ruler and focus buttons. On a phone the same four controls and Contents sit in a bar at the bottom of the screen.

  • The sleekest of the three and the nearest to the original guides.
  • Every control has a word under or beside its icon; there is no grid of unlabelled icons.
  • Weakness: the bar sits over the top of the text, and the book title is shown only in the sidebar.
  • Weakness: the largest change to the layout you approved last round.
