Sidebar and scrolling: three arrangements

Each sketch is a small working model of the Linguistics book, using its real topics and answers. Pick an arrangement below, then scroll and click inside the frame. Dashed outlines mark what scrolls: orange is the contents list, blue is the text. Grey areas never scroll.

Recommended: A. It keeps the reader’s present layout and book identity, fixes the four measured faults, and reuses Language Muscle’s own contents behaviour. B is the choice only if the reader must sit inside other websites without a fixed height. C suits phones and very long books, but is new to these three.

Today, as measured

Search, Home and Notes take the top 199 pixels and stay put. Below them, the cover, title, progress bar and the first topics share one scrolling list, so the topics start about halfway down a 900-pixel screen. Clicking a topic name opens an empty topic page instead of opening the topic.

A · Recommended

Pinned header, one contents list

One short pinned header carries the cover, the book title (which goes to the book’s home page) and Search. Everything below it is the contents list. A topic row opens and closes the topic; only answers open a page. After you pick an answer, the text takes over the keyboard.

B

The page scrolls, the contents stays beside it

The browser page itself scrolls the text, as on a documentation site. The contents column sticks to the side and scrolls on its own. Space, Page Down, Back and find-in-page work as on any web page without the reader steering focus.

C

Topics first, then answers

The contents shows the nine topics. Choosing a topic replaces the list with that topic’s answers and a row back to all topics. The longest list is 45 answers instead of a 253-entry tree.
